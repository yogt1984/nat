**Subject:** Prospective PhD inquiry — a crypto perpetuals dataset and platform, and what it says about risk

Dear Professor Farkas,

I am writing to express my interest in pursuing a PhD at ETH Zürich / UZH. My background is in
embedded systems engineering and quantitative trading; over the past year I built a real-time
research platform for cryptocurrency perpetual futures that computes 236 order-book and flow
features at 100 ms cadence on BTC, ETH and SOL, and I hold the resulting tick-level dataset.

Given that you have supervised theses in this area, the practical offer may be as useful as the
findings: the platform, the dataset, and the infrastructure are available, and several of the
open questions in it are the right size for a master's thesis rather than a doctorate.

On risk specifically, one result may interest you. On a venue that publishes per-position liquidation
prices, I can build the liquidation map exactly rather than estimating it — and measuring it produced
an uncomfortable finding: the wallets holding most of the notional and the wallets that actually get
liquidated are different populations. A registry covering 21.9% of liquidated notional contained only
2.9% of liquidated wallets. Cascade-risk measures built on aggregate open interest and an assumed
leverage mix are therefore mis-specified in a way that a coverage statistic denominated in notional
will not reveal.

The preprints are at [SSRN link] (lead PDF attached, plus a one-page summary of all eight).

welcome any advice on the right route in — I am open to a pre-doctoral or research-assistant position
as readily as to direct doctoral entry.
