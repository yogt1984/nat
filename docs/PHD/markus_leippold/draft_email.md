**Subject:** Prospective PhD inquiry — a signed narrative measure, and what it would take to prove it adds anything

Dear Professor Leippold,

I am writing to express my interest in pursuing a PhD under your supervision at UZH. My background is
in embedded systems engineering and quantitative trading. Over the past year I built two systems: a
real-time crypto perpetual-futures platform computing 236 microstructure features at 100 ms cadence,
and a multi-source news aggregator that produces a continuous narrative measure.

The narrative measure is where I would value your judgement most. *Perception Pressure* is
salience × valence: a non-negative attention term multiplied by a credibility-weighted directional
sentiment term in [−1, +1], so the signal is signed and its magnitude is meaningful. Source diversity
enters as a multiplier rather than a summand, so a single outlet cannot inflate it, and source
credibility is earned through automated cross-corroboration rather than hand-assigned — which is
where I think comparable indices quietly encode the researcher's priors.

I want to be straightforward that no empirical validation exists yet. The companion paper argues at
the level of methodology why an exogenous narrative signal should help a microstructure book —
timescale separation, effective breadth, and partial evasion of an adverse-selection barrier that
caps the order-flow signals — and states each claim as a hypothesis with an explicit kill criterion
and no performance figures. Its weakest point is one I would rather name than hide: the breadth
argument uses a calm-regime correlation, and the binding one is under stress.

Both preprints are at [SSRN link] (PDFs attached, plus a one-page summary of the wider work).

to share the codebase or the data.
