\ophead{From Narrative to Alpha}
{Exogenous Perception Pressure as an Orthogonal Signal in an Autonomous Microstructure Alpha Book:
 Timescale Separation, Effective Breadth, and the Adverse-Selection Barrier}
{\code{prism_signal_preprint.tex} $\cdot$ 9pp $\cdot$ Jul 2026 $\cdot$ theory + protocol,
 \textbf{no performance figures by design}}

\begin{multicols}{2}

\opsec{The problem it names}
A book built entirely from order-book microstructure is powerful but \textbf{structurally narrow}:
\begin{opitems}
\item one information source (order flow),
\item one band of timescales (sub-second to minutes),
\item jointly capped by an adverse-selection barrier that collapses a large unconditional IC once you
      condition on the states where a passive fill actually occurs (\#2, \#7).
\end{opitems}
Question: what does \emph{a single exogenous signal from a different information domain} contribute to
such a book? Answered at the level of methodology, not measured performance.

\opsec{Integration architecture (source-agnostic)}
A narrative series is aligned to market bars by a \textbf{point-in-time as-of join} and then scored by
\emph{the same} IC, MI, and five-gate replication machinery as any endogenous feature. The exogenous
signal gets no privileges — that is the design's credibility.

\opsec{Three reasons it can help without being individually strong}
\textbf{I. Timescale separation.} Narrative momentum lives in a spectral band essentially disjoint from
the $0.005$--$0.1$\,Hz band that carries order-book-imbalance predictability (\#7). The two are close to
orthogonal \emph{by construction}, not by luck.

\textbf{II. Effective breadth.} Under the fundamental law,
\[
  \IR\approx\ICbar\sqrt{\Neff},\qquad \Neff=\frac{N}{1+(N-1)\rhobar}
\]
appending a \emph{low-correlation} signal raises $\Neff$ more than appending another correlated
microstructure signal \emph{of equal strength}. This is the quantitative core: a weak orthogonal signal
can beat a strong redundant one, and the formula says by how much.

\textbf{III. Partial evasion of the adverse-selection barrier.} A slow exogenous signal is harvested by
\emph{patient} execution over its own horizon rather than by reactive spread-crossing, so its coupling to
the fillable-state indicator — the mechanism that destroys microstructure edge — is weaker. Its
conditional IC need not collapse the same way.

\opsec{Status of each claim}
\caution{All three are stated as \textbf{hypotheses with explicit, leakage-controlled, FDR-corrected
tests}. The paper reports \textbf{no performance figures}. Reason III in particular is an argument about
a mechanism, not a measurement — it is the one most likely to be attacked, and the one worth testing
first.}

\opsec{What ``more complete'' means}
The contribution is a theory and a protocol for making an autonomous microstructure research platform
\emph{more complete}: a decorrelated, multi-domain alpha book \textbf{that reports its own honest
effective breadth}, admitting narrative as a first-class, gated signal source rather than an ad-hoc
overlay.

\opsec{Why this matters strategically}
It is the argument for why the platform should not just keep mining order-flow features. Adding the
$n{+}1$-th imbalance variant moves $\Neff$ almost not at all once $\rhobar$ is high; adding one genuinely
exogenous input moves it materially. If the microstructure edge is execution-bounded (and \#2/\#7 say it
is), \textbf{breadth is the remaining lever}.

\opsec{The honest weak point}
$\Neff$ gains are computed with $\rhobar$ estimated in-sample and in calm conditions. \#6 makes the
matching argument that \textbf{the binding correlation is the stress-regime one} — narrative and
microstructure may correlate precisely during the events that matter. Any test must estimate $\rhobar$
conditional on stress, or the breadth gain is an artifact.

\opsec{The feature-source contract}
Design principle: \textbf{the exogenous signal must be indistinguishable, to the scoring machinery, from
an endogenous feature} — held to the same standard, no special pleading. A source exposes
\code{load(symbol, start, end)} returning a frame indexed by UTC timestamp, one column per narrative
component. The process runner merges onto market bars; thereafter a narrative column is scored by
\code{ic_horizon} and \code{mi_ksg} exactly as an order-book column is. \textbf{Processes operate on
columns and never learn a column's origin} — narrative is first-class because it is admitted by the type
system, not by a bespoke code path.
Alignment is a \textbf{backward as-of join}: a bar sees only narrative stamped at or before its close.

\opsec{Evaluation protocol — each with its kill criterion}
\begin{opnums}
\item \textbf{Orthogonality.} Volatility-regime-conditional $\rho_x$ between narrative momentum and the
      incumbent imbalance signal, plus cross-spectral coherence. \emph{Kill:} if $\rho_x$ is not small
      \textbf{in stress regimes}, the breadth argument does not apply at all.
\item \textbf{Incremental content.} $\MI(r_{t\to t+h};\dot P_t \mid \text{price}_t,\text{volume}_t)$ at
      narrative horizons, $k$NN estimator, block-permutation null, BH across concepts and horizons.
      \emph{Kill:} not significantly positive after FDR $\Rightarrow$ narrative adds nothing beyond price
      and volume.
\item \textbf{Breadth realization.} Book $\Neff$ and OOS IR with and without the signal, walk-forward.
      \emph{Kill:} neither rises on inclusion.
\item \textbf{Execution realizability.} Schedule-based execution. \emph{Kill:} conditional IC collapses
      the way the microstructure signals' does.
\end{opnums}
Note the discipline: \textbf{the kill criteria are written before any number exists}, which is what
stops (3) from becoming an exercise in finding a window where $\Neff$ went up.
